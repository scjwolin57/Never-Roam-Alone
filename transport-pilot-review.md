# Transport pilot — 25 cities for format approval

This is the proposed content and layout for the **Getting to and from** cards. If you're happy with this, I'll run the same format across the remaining ~870 cities.

Format rules used:

- Route fields: one line per route — `Destination (Country) — Operator — booking link`, with `[night]` appended for sleepers.
- Notes fields: 2–4 sentences of plain prose — which station/terminal/port, booking quirks, border checks, seasonality.
- Where a mode genuinely doesn't exist, the route field says `None` **and the notes field says why** (e.g. landlocked). Previously these were just blank, which read as missing data.

## Aachen, Germany

**Intl Train Routes**  

Brussels, Liege & Paris (Belgium / France) — Eurostar (former Thalys, Paris-Dortmund route) — https://www.eurostar.com/  
Brussels-Midi & Liege (Belgium) — DB Fernverkehr, ICE International — https://www.bahn.de/en  
Maastricht (Netherlands) — Arriva Nederland, RE 18 — https://www.arriva.nl/  
Welkenraedt, Verviers & Liege (Belgium) — SNCB/NMBS, RE 29 euregioAIXpress — https://www.belgiantrain.be/en  
Heerlen (Netherlands) — DB Regio NRW, RB 20 Euregiobahn — https://www.bahn.de/en

**Domestic Trains**  

Deutsche Bahn (ICE long-distance and DB Regio NRW) — https://www.bahn.de/en  
National Express Germany (RE 1 NRW-Express, RE 4 Wupper-Express) — https://nationalexpress.de/

**Train Notes**  

Every cross-border train uses Aachen Hbf, where the high-speed line from Cologne meets HSL 3 into Belgium; several platforms carry switchable overhead power so they can handle Belgian 3 kV DC as well as German 15 kV AC. Eurostar, the former Thalys, still calls at Aachen on the Paris-Brussels-Liege-Cologne-Dortmund run, and DB's ICE International works Frankfurt-Cologne-Aachen-Liege-Brussels, but the two fare systems are separate - book Eurostar on eurostar.com and the ICE on bahn.de, and do not expect one ticket to be valid on the other. The cheap way over the border is regional: Arriva's RE 18 to Maastricht, SNCB's RE 29 euregioAIXpress to Welkenraedt, Verviers and Liege, and DB's RB 20 Euregiobahn to Heerlen all take ordinary regional tickets. There are no night trains at Aachen, and since every crossing is inside Schengen there are no routine passport checks, though you should still carry photo ID.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

Brussels, Amsterdam, Paris, London, Prague, Warsaw, Milan, Barcelona & beyond — FlixBus — https://global.flixbus.com/bus/aachen

**Bus Notes**  

Aachen has no coach station as such - long-distance buses use the stop at Aachen West station on Henricistrasse, about 2 km northwest of the Hauptbahnhof and a short city-bus ride from the centre. FlixBus lists roughly 95 directly reachable destinations from Aachen, unusually many for a city this size, because it sits on the Ruhr-to-Benelux and Ruhr-to-France corridors. Buy on flixbus.com or in the app; tickets are e-tickets scanned at the door and advance fares can start around 5 euros. Belgian and Dutch runs such as Liege, Maastricht, Brussels and Amsterdam are short Schengen hops with no border formalities, while the London coaches include a full UK immigration check at the Channel, so carry a passport.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

Aachen is an inland city in the far west of Germany with no coast, no navigable river and no lake, so no passenger ferry of any kind operates there.

## Alexandria, Egypt

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

Egyptian National Railways (ENR) — https://enr.gov.eg/

**Train Notes**  

Egypt has no international passenger rail at all - the line west towards Libya is unfinished, Sudan's network is a different gauge with no through connection, and the links east to Gaza and Israel have been severed since 1948 - so every train from Alexandria is domestic. Trains start from Misr Station in the centre but nearly all also call at Sidi Gaber about 3 km east, where the crowd moving for the doors regularly makes visitors get off by mistake; stay aboard unless you actually want the eastern districts. Around 30 services a day run to Cairo Ramses, the fastest taking about 2 hr 40 min, with slower trains to Marsa Matruh and the Delta towns, and the Watania/Abela sleeper to Luxor and Aswan connects through Cairo. Foreigners pay dollar-denominated fares, bookable on the ENR portal at enr.gov.eg or at the station window - the portal needs an account and is awkward with non-Egyptian cards, so buying at the counter a day ahead is the reliable fallback.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

None

**Bus Notes**  

No scheduled international coach starts in Alexandria - the cross-border services into Egypt (JETT from Amman, SAPTCO from Saudi Arabia, and intermittent runs from Libya) all terminate in Cairo, and from Israel you cross on foot at Taba and pick up an Egyptian bus on the far side. Domestically Alexandria is well served by Go Bus (https://go-bus.com/), Blue Bus, Super Jet, West & Middle Delta and Upper Egypt Bus, all running air-conditioned coaches with hostess service. The main terminal is Moharam Bek, with extra pick-up points at Gedid station and, for Red Sea routes, at Sidi Gaber. Cairo takes roughly 3 to 4 hours, but many services start at Almaza in Heliopolis rather than the centre, which adds an hour or more - buy from the company office, never from touts outside your hotel.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

There is no scheduled passenger ferry from Alexandria as of 2026 - the Western Harbour is now a cargo and container operation, and the Mediterranean passenger lines it once had to Greece, Turkey and Italy were withdrawn years ago. Cruise ships call but cannot be booked point to point, and freighter operators are very reluctant to carry passengers out of Egypt. Egypt's only surviving scheduled international sea link is on the Red Sea, where AB Maritime runs Nuweiba to Aqaba in Jordan, while the Aswan to Wadi Halfa ferry into Sudan is closed because of the civil war.

## Almaty, Kazakhstan

**Intl Train Routes**  

Tashkent (Uzbekistan) - via Taraz and Shymkent — Kazakhstan Railways (KTZ) — https://bilet.railways.kz/ [night]  
Novosibirsk (Russia) - via Aktogai and Semey — Kazakhstan Railways (KTZ) — https://bilet.railways.kz/ [night]

**Domestic Trains**  

Kazakhstan Railways (KTZ / Qazaqstan Temir Zholy) — https://bilet.railways.kz/

**Train Notes**  

All mainline trains use Almaty-2 station at the north end of downtown, with northbound services also calling at Almaty-1 out towards the airport. KTZ runs a sleeper to Tashkent on alternate days, about 16 hours via Taraz and Shymkent, and a direct sleeper to Novosibirsk in Russia on odd dates; for Moscow you normally change at Astana, Saratov or Petropavl rather than riding a single through train, so check what is actually selling before you plan around it. The Almaty-Urumqi service into China was cancelled during the pandemic and has still not resumed. Carry your passport for overnight border checks, and be aware the KTZ booking site bilet.railways.kz is mostly in Kazakh and Russian.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

Bishkek & Karakol (Kyrgyzstan) — Sayran Bus Station e-ticketing — https://ma-sairan.kz/  
Tashkent (Uzbekistan) — Sayran Bus Station e-ticketing — https://ma-sairan.kz/  
Yining, Urumqi & Khorgos (China) — Sayran Bus Station e-ticketing — https://ma-sairan.kz/

**Bus Notes**  

International coaches and shared minibuses use Sayran bus station about 8 km west of the centre, and its own site ma-sairan.kz publishes the timetable and sells tickets online, which is unusual for the region. Bishkek is the busiest run at roughly 4-5 hours with five departures a day via the Korday border, Karakol takes about 8 hours, Tashkent about 14 hours twice daily, and early-morning buses cross to Yining (12 hr) and Urumqi (19 hr) in China. Most Western nationalities enter Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan visa-free, but China requires a visa arranged well in advance. The old Sayakhat terminal has been demolished, though intercity taxis and marshrutkas may still drop you at that junction rather than at Sayran.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

Almaty is landlocked; there are no ferries. Kazakhstan's only international sea link is the Caspian ferry from Aktau in the far west to Baku, Azerbaijan.

## Bergen, Norway

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

Vy (Bergensbanen, Bergen - Voss - Myrdal - Oslo) — https://www.vy.no/  
Flamsbana / Flam Railway (Myrdal - Flam) — https://www.flamsbana.no/

**Train Notes**  

All trains use Bergen stasjon on Stromgaten, a short walk from the harbour and sharing a forecourt with the bus station. Vy's Bergensbanen runs several times daily to Oslo in about 6 hr 45 min plus a nightly sleeper, and the cheapest minipris fares are released roughly three months out on vy.no, so booking on the day is expensive. The Flam Railway connects at Myrdal and Vy sells a through-ticket covering both legs; if you are carrying a bicycle for the Rallarvegen you must add a bike ticket when you book. There are no international trains from Bergen at all - the nearest cross-border services to Sweden start from Oslo, so any rail trip abroad means riding the Bergen Line east first.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

None

**Bus Notes**  

Intercity coaches leave from Bergen busstasjon on Stromgaten, immediately beside the railway station. NOR-WAY Bussekspress runs the main network, including NW400 Kystbussen to Haugesund and Stavanger, NW180 Haukeliekspressen over the mountains to Oslo, NW420 Sognefjordekspressen to Sogndal and NW431 Fjordekspressen towards Forde and Nordfjord; buy at nor-way.no or in the app, and reservations are rarely essential outside holidays. Local and regional buses around the Bergen region are run separately by Skyss (https://www.skyss.no/). No scheduled international coach serves Bergen, so for Sweden, Denmark or the Continent you connect via Oslo.

**Ferry Routes**  

Hirtshals (Denmark) - via Stavanger, from Jekteviksterminalen — Fjord Line — https://www.fjordline.com/en [night]  
Kirkenes (Norway) - coastal voyage calling at 34 ports — Hurtigruten — https://www.hurtigruten.com/en  
Kirkenes (Norway) - coastal voyage — Havila Voyages — https://www.havilavoyages.com/  
Stavanger (Norway) - from Jekteviksterminalen — Fjord Line — https://www.fjordline.com/en  
Flam (Norway) - Sognefjord express boat — Norled — https://www.norled.no/en/

**Ferry Notes**  

Fjord Line uses Jekteviksterminalen, close enough to walk from the city centre, and its Hirtshals service runs year-round via Risavika in Stavanger; the crossing takes about 17.5 hours overnight, so a cabin or reclining Airseat is compulsory rather than optional. The same two ships, MS Stavangerfjord and MS Bergensfjord, cover the domestic Bergen to Stavanger leg in roughly 6.5 hours with no passport needed. Hurtigruten and Havila Voyages both sail the coastal route north to Kirkenes, calling at 34 ports over about six days each way; these are working coastal ships as well as cruises, so port-to-port tickets are sold alongside the full round trip. Norled's Sognefjord express boat to Flam runs roughly April to October and takes about 5.5 hours, and no ferry connects Bergen with the UK, Shetland or the Faroes any more.

## Busan, South Korea

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

KORAIL (KTX) — https://www.korail.com/global/eng/main

**Train Notes**  

There are no international trains from South Korea - the sealed border with North Korea blocks all overland rail. Domestically, KORAIL's KTX high-speed trains run from Busan Station to Seoul in about 2 hr 30 min; book in English on the KORAIL Global site. SRT high-speed services also link Busan with Suseo in southern Seoul. Busan Station is central, right by the port, so ferry connections are an easy walk or short taxi ride.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

None

**Bus Notes**  

No international buses are possible from South Korea because of the closed North Korean border. Domestic express coaches leave from Busan's Nopo (Central) and Sasang (Seobu) terminals - the main operator is KOBUS/Express Bus (https://www.kobus.co.kr/main.do). Nopo is on Busan Metro Line 1, so getting there without a taxi is straightforward.

**Ferry Routes**  

Fukuoka/Hakata (Japan) — Camellia Line — https://www.camellia-line.co.jp/  
Shimonoseki (Japan) — Kampu Ferry — https://www.kampuferry.co.jp/passenger/english/ [night]  
Osaka (Japan) — PanStar Cruise — https://www.panstar.co.kr/en [night]

**Ferry Notes**  

All international sailings leave from the Busan Port International Passenger Terminal near Busan Station; the Shimonoseki and Osaka services are overnight crossings and the Fukuoka boat runs both overnight and daytime, so carry your passport and allow time for immigration. Fast day boats to Tsushima (Japan) are run by Korean operators such as Miraejet and Daea. JR Kyushu's Queen Beetle fast ferry to Fukuoka was permanently withdrawn in December 2024, so the Camellia Line ship is now the main Busan-Fukuoka link.

## Cape Town, South Africa

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

Metrorail Western Cape (PRASA commuter network) — https://cttrains.co.za/  
The Blue Train (Cape Town - Pretoria luxury service) — https://www.bluetrain.co.za/ [night]  
Rovos Rail (Cape Town - Pretoria luxury service) — https://rovos.com/ [night]

**Train Notes**  

No international trains reach Cape Town, and PRASA's long-distance Shosholoza Meyl services, including Cape Town to Johannesburg, remain suspended with no confirmed restart as of mid-2026. What still runs from Cape Town station is Metrorail's commuter network - the Simon's Town line along False Bay, hugging the sea from Muizenberg south, is one of the world's great suburban rides - plus the luxury Blue Train and Rovos Rail to Pretoria, which need booking months ahead. Metrorail's own web presence is patchy, so most locals use the free community-run timetable site cttrains.co.za to plan trips. Most Metrorail ticket offices take cash only, and it is wise to avoid travelling after dark.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

Windhoek (Namibia) — Intercape — https://intercape.co.za/

**Bus Notes**  

Intercape's Windhoek coach takes around 21 hours with passport formalities at the Vioolsdrif/Noordoewer border, and Baileys Reo Liner also runs a Namibia service a few days a week. Long-distance coaches leave from the terminal on the Adderley Street side of Cape Town railway station, by the Golden Acre building. Greyhound (https://greyhound.co.za/) and Intercape cover the domestic network to Johannesburg, Durban and the Garden Route, while the hop-on hop-off Baz Bus serves backpacker hostels along the coast.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

No scheduled international passenger ferries call at Cape Town - only cruise ships at the V&A Waterfront cruise terminal, plus the occasional passenger-carrying cargo sailing towards St Helena. Local boats run from the Waterfront to Robben Island.

## Cartagena, Colombia

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

None

**Train Notes**  

Colombia has no scheduled passenger railway serving Cartagena; the only surviving passenger trains are tourist operations near Bogota such as the Tren de la Sabana and Turistren, none of which reach the Caribbean coast.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

None

**Bus Notes**  

Every intercity coach uses the Terminal de Transportes de Cartagena on the Carretera de la Cordialidad in Barrio Pozon, a good 20 to 30 minutes east of the walled city by taxi or on the Transcaribe feeder, so allow far more time than the distance suggests. Expreso Brasilia (https://www.expresobrasilia.com/), Berlinas del Fonce, Copetran, Unitransco and Rapido Ochoa share the domestic network; typical runs are Barranquilla in about 2 hours, Santa Marta in 4 to 5, Medellin in around 13 and Bogota in roughly 20, with overnight servicio de lujo coaches on the Bogota and Bucaramanga routes. No operator sells a scheduled through-coach across a border from Cartagena: for Venezuela you travel to Maicao or Cucuta and cross on foot, and there is no road link at all to Panama because of the Darien Gap.

**Ferry Routes**  

San Blas Islands & Portobelo area (Panama) - shared sailing passage from Cartagena's Manga marinas — Blue Sailing — https://bluesailing.net/en_us/

**Ferry Notes**  

Day boats to the Islas del Rosario, Baru and Playa Blanca leave from the Muelle Turistico de la Bodeguita next to the walled city, generally between about 07:30 and 09:00 with returns mid-afternoon, but they are run by many small licensed lancha operators and sold at the dock or through hotels rather than by a single ferry company, with a port tax and marine park fee collected on the day. Crossings run from about 45 minutes to 1.5 hours depending on the island, and the December to February windy season brings choppy seas and cancellations. The Cartagena to Panama passage sold by Blue Sailing is a five-night shared sailing charter through the San Blas islands rather than a scheduled ferry, it carries no vehicles, and berths sell out weeks ahead. Similarly named Colombia-to-Panama boat trips such as San Blas Adventures actually start from Capurgana and Sapzurro, not Cartagena, and no scheduled passenger or vehicle ferry currently links Cartagena with Panama.

## Chiang Mai, Thailand

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

State Railway of Thailand (SRT) — https://dticket.railway.co.th/

**Train Notes**  

Chiang Mai is the northern terminus of the State Railway of Thailand and no train from here crosses a border - Thailand's international rail links are far away at Padang Besar for Malaysia and Nong Khai for Laos. The station is about 3 km east of the old city, and services run to Bangkok's Krung Thep Aphiwat in 12 to 15 hours, with two daytime trains and around four overnight sleepers. Second-class air-conditioned sleepers sell out first, so book ahead; tickets go on sale up to 180 days out. From outside Thailand use the official SRT D-Ticket site, which needs an account, rejects special characters in the registration form, requires booking at least three days ahead and sells only first and second-class air-con sleepers.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

None

**Bus Notes**  

Long-distance coaches use Arcade Bus Station (Terminals 2 and 3) on Kaeo Nawarat Road northeast of the old city, with Green Bus (https://web.greenbusthailand.com/en) covering the whole north and even a 24-hour run to Phuket, and Nakhonchai Air and the state BKS operator running to Bangkok in about 9 to 12 hours. No scheduled international coach leaves Chiang Mai under a single bookable operator: for Laos take a Green Bus or minibus to Chiang Khong (about 6 hours), cross the Fourth Thai-Lao Friendship Bridge on the shuttle, and pick up a Lao bus at Huay Xai, where PLS Company runs services on into Laos and China. Through Chiang Mai to Luang Prabang tickets of roughly 18 hours are sold by agents in town, but they are agency products rather than one carrier's route. Most nationalities get a Lao visa on arrival at the bridge; bring US dollars in cash and a passport photo.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

Chiang Mai is inland in the northern mountains, so there are no sea ferries and the only boats in town are sightseeing and dinner cruises on the Ping River. The classic water route out of the region is the Mekong slow boat, which leaves from Huay Xai in Laos - about 6 hours away by bus to Chiang Khong plus the border crossing - and takes two days downriver to Luang Prabang with an overnight stop at Pakbeng. Departures moved to around 09:00, so you normally have to overnight in Chiang Khong or Huay Xai first rather than connecting the same day. More comfortable two-day cruises on the same stretch are run by Shompoo Cruise (https://shompoo-cruise.com/en/) and Nagi of Mekong.

## Colombo, Sri Lanka

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

Sri Lanka Railways — https://seatreservation.railway.gov.lk/

**Train Notes**  

Sri Lanka is an island, so there are no international trains. Sri Lanka Railways runs scenic domestic lines from Colombo Fort station - notably the hill-country route to Kandy, Nanu Oya (for Nuwara Eliya) and Ella, and the coastal line to Galle and Matara. Reserve seats online in advance for popular trains, as observation-car and first-class seats on the hill line go quickly.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

None

**Bus Notes**  

No international coaches operate, because Sri Lanka has no land borders. Domestic long-distance buses run by the state SLTB and private operators leave from the Bastian Mawatha and Pettah terminals in Colombo for Kandy, Jaffna, Galle and across the island. Expressway coaches on the Southern and Central expressways are the fastest option and are bought at the terminal counter.

**Ferry Routes**  

Nagapattinam (India) - departs Kankesanthurai, far northern Sri Lanka — IndSri Ferry Services [seasonal]

**Ferry Notes**  

The only international passenger boat is the on-again, off-again Kankesanthurai (KKS) to Nagapattinam ferry, which sails from KKS near Jaffna - roughly 400 km north of Colombo, reached by train or bus. It has been suspended repeatedly since its 2023 launch because of rough seas and commercial problems, so confirm it is actually running before planning around it. Carry your passport and an Indian visa; the operator has no reliable public booking site, so tickets are arranged through agents. Colombo's own port handles cargo and cruise calls only.

## Cusco, Peru

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

PeruRail — https://book.perurail.com  
Inca Rail — https://incarail.com/en

**Train Notes**  

Machu Picchu trains mostly no longer start in central Cusco: both PeruRail and Inca Rail sell a bimodal bus-plus-train product from Cusco, and since 1 September 2025 PeruRail has used Pachar as the transfer point on selected departures rather than Ollantaytambo. Many travellers skip the packaged bus and take a colectivo straight to Ollantaytambo station, which has the fullest timetable; Poroy on the city's edge is no longer in regular use and San Pedro in Cusco itself serves mainly the Hidroelectrica local run. PeruRail also works the southern line to Puno with the daytime Titicaca train and the Belmond Andean Explorer sleeper, which continues to Arequipa. Peru runs no international passenger trains, so nothing crosses from Cusco to Bolivia, Chile or Brazil by rail, and Machu Picchu tickets should be booked well ahead in the June to August peak because train seats and entry slots sell out together.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

Copacabana & La Paz (Bolivia) — Bolivia Hop — https://bushop.com/bolivia/

**Bus Notes**  

Long-distance coaches use the Terminal Terrestre de Cusco in the Santiago district, about 15 minutes by taxi from the Plaza de Armas, and a small terminal fee is payable before boarding. Cruz del Sur (https://www.cruzdelsur.com.pe/), Oltursa (https://www.oltursa.pe/), Civa and Turismo Mer cover the domestic network to Lima in around 21 hours, Arequipa in about 10, Puno in roughly 7 and Abancay in 5. Bolivia Hop and its sister Peru Hop run hop-on hop-off coaches through to Copacabana and La Paz, crossing at Kasani or Desaguadero with staff on board to walk passengers through the exit and entry stamps, which is the main reason travellers pay a premium over local buses. Local carriers also sell Cusco to La Paz through-tickets at the terminal, usually 12 hours or more with a change in Puno; most nationalities get a Bolivian stamp on arrival, but US citizens should check visa requirements first.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

Cusco is a landlocked Andean city at about 3,400 m with no navigable water of its own, and the nearest passenger boats are the Lake Titicaca launches to the Uros, Taquile and Amantani islands, which leave from Puno some seven hours away by road.

## Da Nang, Vietnam

**Intl Train Routes**  

None

**Domestic Trains**  

Vietnam Railways — https://dsvn.vn

**Train Notes**  

Da Nang Railway Station sits on the North-South Reunification line, and there are no international rail services from anywhere in central Vietnam - the country's only cross-border train runs from Hanoi to Nanning. SE and TN trains run north to Hue in about 2.5 hours, one of Vietnam's most scenic rail stretches as the line clings to the coast over the Hai Van Pass, then on to Hanoi in roughly 16 to 17 hours, and south to Ho Chi Minh City in about 17 hours. Book on dsvn.vn or at the station; soft-sleeper berths on the overnight trains sell out around Tet and the summer holidays.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

Savannakhet, Pakse & Vientiane (Laos) - west through the Lao Bao border — Vietnamese and Lao cross-border operators, tickets via VeXeRe — https://vexere.com/en-US

**Bus Notes**  

Long-distance services use the Da Nang Central Bus Station a few kilometres out of town, served by city bus 2. Open-tour buses and shuttles run constantly to Hoi An (about 45 min) and to Hue (about 2.5 to 3 hr, though most use the tunnel rather than the Hai Van Pass), while sleeper coaches link Da Nang with Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City overnight. Cross-border coaches head west through the Lao Bao checkpoint to Savannakhet, Pakse and Vientiane in Laos; these are run by small Vietnamese and Lao companies rather than one carrier, so book at the station, through your accommodation or on the VeXeRe platform. Most nationalities can get a Lao visa on arrival at Lao Bao, but bring US dollars in cash and a passport photo.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

Da Nang's port handles cargo and cruise ships rather than scheduled passenger ferries; the main on-water options for visitors are Han River dinner and sightseeing cruises.

## George Town, Malaysia

**Intl Train Routes**  

Hat Yai & Bangkok (Thailand) - change at Padang Besar, no through train — State Railway of Thailand (SRT) — https://dticket.railway.co.th/ [night]

**Domestic Trains**  

KTM Berhad (ETS and Komuter Utara, from Butterworth) — https://www.ktmb.com.my/

**Train Notes**  

Penang Island has no railway station: trains use Butterworth on the mainland, a few minutes' walk from the ferry terminal inside the Penang Sentral complex. KTM's ETS runs about seven times a day from Kuala Lumpur via Ipoh, with one train each from Segamat and Johor Bahru; from Singapore you take the Causeway shuttle to Johor Bahru and change at Gemas. There is no longer any through train from Bangkok - take a Thai sleeper to Padang Besar, clear the co-located Thai and Malaysian immigration there (get off at the Malaysian station, not the Thai one), then continue on a KTM Komuter Utara service to Butterworth. Reserve a week or more ahead on ktmb.com.my, which now also sells State Railway of Thailand tickets; note that Easybook is no longer an authorised KTMB agent.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

Hat Yai (Thailand) & Singapore (Singapore) — Sri Maju Group — https://www.srimaju.com/  
Hat Yai (Thailand) & Singapore (Singapore) - from Penang Sentral, Butterworth — multiple operators via Penang Sentral e-ticketing — https://www.penangsentral.com.my/

**Bus Notes**  

Island-side coaches use the Sungai Nibong terminal about fifteen minutes south of George Town, but the bigger hub is Penang Sentral in Butterworth, next to the train station and ferry terminal. Penang Sentral's e-ticketing covers more than fifty domestic destinations plus the only two international ones, Hat Yai and Singapore. Hat Yai is roughly 5 hours with a stop at Bukit Kayu Hitam where you clear Malaysian and Thai immigration in person, and Singapore takes about 10 hours. Minivans to Krabi, Phuket, Trang and Surat Thani are sold by agents around Penang Road, and the terminal warns explicitly against buying express bus tickets from touts.

**Ferry Routes**  

Butterworth (Malaysia) - Raja Tun Uda terminal at Weld Quay, George Town — Penang Ferry Service (Penang Port) — https://www.penangport.com.my/

**Ferry Notes**  

The Penang Ferry Service, the oldest in Malaysia, crosses the 3 km strait between Raja Tun Uda terminal at Weld Quay in George Town and Sultan Abdul Halim terminal at Butterworth in about 15 minutes for RM1.20 each way. Sailings run roughly 06:30 to 20:30 from the mainland and 07:00 to 21:00 from the island, and since the car ferries were retired in 2020 the boats carry only pedestrians, bicycles and motorcycles. There is no longer a Penang to Langkawi ferry - Langkawi Ferry Services withdrew it, and Langkawi Ferry Line now sails only from Kuala Kedah and Kuala Perlis, both a few hours north by road. The Penang to Medan ferry into Indonesia is also long gone, so Swettenham Pier in George Town now handles cruise ships only.

## Hanoi, Vietnam

**Intl Train Routes**

Baku (Azerbaijan) — Georgian Railway / Azerbaijan Railways (ADY) — https://ticket.ady.az/en [night]

**Domestic Trains**  

Georgian Railway — https://tickets.railway.ge/en

**Train Notes**  

Both international services are overnight sleepers from Tbilisi Central (Tbilisi Sadguri), on the north side of the city at the Station Square metro stop. The Yerevan train runs every second day, becoming nightly in the summer season from about mid-June to late September and less often in winter. The Baku overnight train has relaunched but is currently usable only by travellers who can enter Azerbaijan visa-free, which excludes many Western nationals, so check your eligibility before buying. Domestic trains, including the fast Stadler service to Batumi and the branch to Zugdidi for Svaneti, are booked on Georgian Railway's own ticketing site and sell out at weekends.

**Intl Bus Routes**  

Istanbul (Turkey) — Metro Turizm — https://www.metroturizm.com.tr/

**Bus Notes**  

Metro Turizm runs long-distance coaches between Tbilisi and Istanbul, roughly a day-long trip along the Black Sea coast and through the Sarpi border crossing. To Yerevan and Baku, transport is mostly frequent shared minibuses (marshrutkas) from the Ortachala and Avlabari stations rather than a bookable coach line, so you buy on the spot and leave when the vehicle fills. Carry your passport for all border crossings, and note that the Russian border at Larsi is subject to long queues and closures. Journey times are about 6 hours to Yerevan and 8-9 to Baku.

**Ferry Routes**  

None

**Ferry Notes**  

Tbilisi is inland with no ferries; international Black Sea passenger and vehicle ferries toward Bulgaria, Romania and occasionally Ukraine sail from Batumi and Poti on Georgia's coast, several hours west, and run to irregular cargo-led schedules, so contact the operator directly before relying on one.
