# Four hotel programs fit the site today; disclosure and link tags must change first

*Research date 2026-09-24. Built from four research notes (OTA programs, networks and aggregators, chains and metasearch, legal compliance) and the site audit of the same date. Every figure carries its source and the date its source shows. "Secondary" marks a blog, directory or search snippet rather than the program's own page. Nothing inside a network dashboard (CJ, Awin, Impact, Partnerize logins) was read, so rates should be re-checked at application time.*

## The answer in one paragraph

Never Roam Alone can join hotel affiliate programs now, with plain tracked links and no page script, but two site changes come first. Four programs fit the site as it stands: **Expedia Group's Travel Creator Program** (direct, 4% on hotel stays, 7-day window, US$30 monthly minimum, clear written terms), **Booking.com through CJ Affiliate** (4% and up on completed stays, session-based, the only route Booking's own page offers), **Agoda Partners** (direct, accepts any country and any size of site, 4 to 5% by tier, but a US$200 minimum and a six-month no-bookings termination clause), and **Hostelworld through Partnerize** for the budget-tier hostel picks (30-day window, rate not published). Hotel chains with a stated deep-link route (Marriott on Partnerize, Hilton on CJ, Accor on CJ) are worth adding later for the chain properties the site already names. Skyscanner is out of reach (5,000 unique visitors a month). Stay22 is the only "one account covers every OTA" option, but it takes up to 70% of the commission and its main products are page scripts; it is a fallback, not a first choice. Travelpayouts and Hotellook were dropped by Jeff on 2026-09-18 and are not recommended. Before any hotel link goes live the site must add a plain-words commission disclosure at the top of every hotel block, put `rel="sponsored"` on every affiliate link (including the live Airalo links), extend privacy.html to name each network, and extend terms.html section 8. No program read requires a cookie banner while links stay plain; a banner comes back the moment a partner script runs on a page.

## Booking.com now lives on CJ, and small publishers were cut once already

Booking.com's own affiliate page no longer offers a direct sign-up; it says "Select your region and complete your registration with CJ" and names no commission, no eligibility rule and no terms link ([Booking.com affiliate page](https://www.booking.com/affiliate-program/v2/index.html), read 2026-09-24). The CJ listing is the primary source for the numbers: commission "starts at 4% for completed accommodation stays", the programme "is session based", applicants need "an active website, blog or social media profile", and "Booking.com will not accept publishers to the programme with social media only profiles" ([CJ: Booking.com](https://www.cj.com/en-gb/publisher/partners/booking.com)). Tracking has no cookie at all: "Booking.com does not use cookies to track affiliate reservations. Always make sure your AID is included in the URL" ([Booking.com KB, 31 Aug 2026](https://affiliates.support.booking.com/kb/s/article/Guidelines-for-Compliant-Use-of-Booking-com-Affiliate-Services)). That is good for this site (nothing runs on the page) and bad for conversion (a reader who comes back tomorrow earns nothing).

The region routing is unsettled. The CJ listing names North America among its eligible regions, while secondary blogs say the Americas went through Awin ([Mize, secondary](https://mize.tech/blog/all-about-the-booking-com-affiliate-partner-program-for-travel-agents/)), and a September 2026 blog reports an Awin email that the Booking program on Awin ended 2026-06-30 ([Kosupa Travel, secondary](https://en.kosupatravel.com/entry/2026/09/15/booking-com-affiliate-program-ends-on-awin-why-im-not-worried-why-stay22-could-be-the-game-changer-june-2026)). The practical rule is to start the CJ application and follow where it routes.

The history matters for a site with no traffic. In May 2025 Booking sent 30-day termination notices to thousands of direct partners, effective June 20, 2025, citing a shift in "strategic focus"; small publishers and bloggers were the ones cut ([Skift, 30 May 2025](https://skift.com/2025/05/30/why-booking-com-cut-thousands-of-affiliate-partners-and-what-comes-next/)). A secondary source puts the cut at partners "earning less than €1000 per month" ([Tragento, secondary](https://tragento.com/en/booking-com-je-izneneda-otkazao-tisuce-affiliate-partnera-sto-sad/)). The direct Partner Centre still exists for kept or invited partners, and its agreement was updated in May 2026 with an AI-training ban, a new Appendix 2 of Compliance Requirements whose breach is material, and a set-off right ([Booking.com KB](https://affiliates.support.booking.com/kb/s/article/Guidelines-for-Compliant-Use-of-Booking-com-Affiliate-Services)). The KB's compliance rules the site must live by: no copying content from Booking.com "or its partner websites, including Agoda, Priceline, Kayak", no redirects or iframes, no imitation sites, no contacting Booking's supply partners, no "Booking" in a domain name, and no vouchers (CJ listing). None of these describes the site today.

Sign-up is direct at creator.expediagroup.com, with tracking and payment through Partnerize ([Partnerize sign-up for Expedia](https://join.partnerize.com/expedia)). Two secondary sources say the program runs on Impact; the primary Partnerize sign-up page contradicts that, so treat "Impact" as wrong until seen in a dashboard.

The live commission page is unambiguous: **Hotel 4%, Activity 4%, Vacation Rental 2%, Package 2%, Car 1.5%, Cruise and Flight 0%**, as a percent of net transaction value on completed bookings, with a **7-day cookie** ([Expedia commission terms](https://creator.expediagroup.com/commission-terms)). Older snippets that mention 6% cruise or US$2 flights are the previous schedule and should be ignored. Payout is **£20 / US$30 / €30 per currency per month**, by direct bank transfer only (no PayPal), and money arrives **60 to 150 days after the trip completes** ([Expedia Payments 101](https://help.creator.expediagroup.com/hc/en-us/articles/13163964169495-Payments-101); [Can I add PayPal](https://help.creator.expediagroup.com/hc/en-us/articles/33222708342551-Can-I-add-PayPal-as-a-payment-option)). Vrbo rides on the same account at the 2% vacation-rental rate.

The clauses that touch this site: 9.1.2 requires a privacy policy that discloses "how you and third parties...track, collect, use" data, and 21.1 makes a privacy breach material; 9.2.2(c) requires FTC compliance; 10.1 requires "original work" and 10.1(g) that the accommodation described "is the same as the one linked to"; 11.1.1 bans linking "indirectly to the Expedia Sites via intermediate site"; 11.1.18 bans iframes; 11.1.21 and 11.1.22 ban scraping and any "extraction, repurposing and/or aggregation" of Travel Content; 13.1 grants Expedia a perpetual licence over content the publisher submits to Expedia ([Expedia T&Cs](https://creator.expediagroup.com/doc/affiliate)). The one unconfirmed acceptance claim, "a minimum of 10 followers", comes from a third-party directory and the official help centre was 403 ([Otto AI, secondary](https://joinotto.com/en-ug/influencer-programs/expedia-group)).

## Agoda accepts anyone but pays slowly and can drop an idle account

Agoda is the friendliest on paper for a site with no audience: "We work with a variety of sites, from small individual web pages to large commercial sites and there are no restrictions on where you are based", with only one hard condition, "You must have a website before you sign up" ([Agoda FAQ](https://partners.agoda.com/en-us/faq.html)). The agreement (v.2023, the live PDF) sets commission by monthly departures: **1 to 50 = 4%, 51 to 300 = 4.5%, 301 and up = 5%**, calculated as (booking value minus taxes and fees) times the rate, and only on a "Materialized Transaction", a stay that actually happened ([Agoda agreement PDF](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf)). The FAQ's "up to 7%" is not in that table; a newer schedule may sit behind the login.

Three terms weigh against applying now. The **US$200 minimum**, paid by bank transfer the month after the guest departs with a **US$10 fee** on international transfers ([Agoda FAQ](https://partners.agoda.com/en-us/faq.html)); clause **8.1.4**, which lets Agoda terminate "in the event no Materialized Transactions have occurred in a period of 6 consecutive months"; and clause **4.2.1**, "ensure a reasonable look-to-book ratio and avoid excessive low quality traffic" ([Agoda agreement PDF](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf)). Tracking is by "HTTP referrer" on a registered domain plus the CID in the link ([Agoda FAQ](https://partners.agoda.com/en-us/faq.html)); the site's Referrer-Policy header was not checked in this pass and must not be "no-referrer" for these links.

One clause deserves a written question before signing. Clause 4.4.1 bars "Paid Search, SEM or SEO activities" in respect of Agoda marks and "Hotel Brands (unless the owner of such Hotel Brand has given its prior written consent)". Read literally it targets advertising campaigns, not editorial pages, but this site ranks pages that name hotels ([Agoda agreement PDF](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf)). Clause 4.8 (price comparison via API only, Agoda ranked at least as favourably as the best converter) applies only if the site "offers Price Comparison" between two or more booking platforms; one researched range plus one link per property is not that. Clause 1.2.2 bans any claim to be "an official partner of Agoda", and governing law is Singapore.

## Hostelworld, Trip.com and the rest of the OTA field

Hostelworld runs on Partnerize; commission is earned "for converted bookings made within 30 days of the first click", last-click among programme participants, and Partnerize, not Hostelworld, pays the affiliate ([Hostelworld partners page](https://partners.hostelworld.com/); [Hostelworld T&Cs PDF](https://partners.hostelworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/hostelworld-affiliate-programme-tcs.pdf)). No primary page states the rate. Directories say **18 to 22%**, but of Hostelworld's own deposit or commission rather than the room price, with about a **US$30** threshold ([Cuelinks, secondary](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/hostelworld-affiliate-program); [UpPromote, secondary](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-program-directory/hostelworld/)). The "Direct Tracked Link" is a plain URL with the affiliate ID; it may not be used on "meta search sites, travel search sites or price comparison sites". Schedule 1 bans brand bidding outright, and the "Affiliate Site" definition covers email lists, which matters only if a newsletter starts ([Hostelworld T&Cs PDF](https://partners.hostelworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/hostelworld-affiliate-programme-tcs.pdf)). It fits the budget tier of the site's three-tier hotel picks and nothing else.

Trip.com is the easiest join and the longest window: "7% Max. basic commission", "30 Day Website cookie window" (7 days in the app), and "Choose any Trip.com page you like. Generate affiliate link in one click" ([Trip.com partners page](https://www.trip.com/partners/index)). A directory puts the hotel default at 5.5% ([UpPromote, secondary](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-program-directory/trip-com/)). Its FAQ and policy pages are script-rendered, so payout threshold, methods and the prohibited-practices list were never read; it is a second-wave add-on, not a first application.

Priceline reached no primary page (3 to 5%, 30-day cookie, via CJ, all secondary). HotelPlanner is a negotiated rev-share or white-label deal ([HotelPlanner partner page](https://www.hotelplanner.com/Affiliate.htm)). Klook, Traveloka and MakeMyTrip run on Asian sub-networks and matter little for a US publisher. Airbnb closed its program in March 2021 ([Hospitable](https://hospitable.com/airbnb-affiliate-program)); Google Hotels has no publisher program at all ([Google Hotel Center help](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/9238463?hl=en)); HotelsCombined's program paused 2020-03-18 ([Travelpayouts blog, secondary](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/hotelscombined-affiliate-program-alternatives/)). Hotellook is reachable only through Travelpayouts and its hotel widget is gone; both are dropped by Jeff 2026-09-18 and not recommended.

## Stay22 is one account for every OTA, at up to 70% of the commission

Stay22 shows Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, Kayak, GetYourGuide and Vrbo logos, calls its tools "free to use", and states no traffic minimum ([stay22.com](https://www.stay22.com/); [Stay22 FAQ](https://www.stay22.com/faq)). The publisher's share "starts at 30%" of what Stay22 earns from the OTA, rising with the site's traffic; Stay22 argues this is "the same or more than you would receive working directly" because it sits on higher OTA volume tiers ([Stay22 FAQ](https://www.stay22.com/faq)). That claim is Stay22's own and was not verified; bloggers report averages near **US$10 a booking** ([Helena Bradbury, secondary](https://www.helenabradbury.com/blog-1/stay22-review-blogger-commission-stats)). Payout is through Tipalti (PayPal, ACH, wire); a commission is "ready" 7 days after checkout and is paid within 30 days of a request; the **US$100** minimum comes from a search excerpt of Stay22's community page, so it is secondary ([Tipalti customer story](https://tipalti.com/resources/customer-stories/stay22/); [Stay22 community](https://community.stay22.com/wheres-my-money)). No cookie window is disclosed anywhere.

The integration question decides it for this site. Allez is described as "Turn any destination into a monetized affiliate link. No code, no API key, free." ([dev.stay22.com/docs](https://dev.stay22.com/docs)), which would be a plain link; but the Quickstart was not fetched, so whether an Allez link resolves a hotel name rather than a destination is unknown. Everything else Stay22 sells (Let Me Allez LinkSwap, Nova, Spark, Maps) is a JavaScript install ([Let Me Allez](https://www.stay22.com/letmeallez)), which would need a CSP allow-list and would set identifiers on the site's own pages, the exact situation that led to removing the Travelpayouts script and the cookie banner on 2026-09-18. The terms (2025-11-11) put all consent duties on the publisher and contain no disclosure, cloaking or cookie-window clause ([Stay22 terms](https://www.stay22.com/terms)). Verdict: fallback only, in link mode only, and only if the direct applications are refused.

## Networks: CJ is unavoidable, Awin optional, Impact already open

CJ is the gate for Booking.com and, for a US publisher, for Hilton, Accor, Best Western and Tripadvisor. Its Publisher Service Agreement could not be fetched (both cj.com/legal URLs 404), so the two clauses that matter come from search excerpts and are secondary: the privacy policy must "fully and accurately disclose their use of third party technology, including CJ's tracking technology, use of cookies and options for discontinuing use of such cookies", and the site must give "prominent notice to visitors about device tracking technologies operated by CJ, provide visitors a link to CJ's privacy notice" and obtain consents "prior to serving tracking technologies" as applicable law requires ([CJ Junction disclosure article, via search excerpts](https://junction.cj.com/article/disclosures-what-you-need-know)). With plain links CJ serves no tracking on the site, so the practical duty is the privacy.html paragraph plus a link. The commonly cited US$50 direct-deposit threshold is not confirmed here.

Awin charges a **£5 / US$5 deposit**, refunded "once you reach your first payment threshold", runs identity verification and manual review, and aims to process applications "within 24 hours" ([Awin joining page](https://www.awin.com/gb/compliance-and-regulations/application-process-and-joining-fee)). ShareASale has been folded into Awin, with a **30-day default window** on most programs ([Awin news](https://www.awin.com/us/news-and-events/awin-news/shareasale-to-awin-upgrade)). Awin is needed only for Hilton UK, Radisson, Minor/NH, Melia and trivago; it is no longer the Booking route (secondary).

Impact is already open through Airalo and has the friendliest floor: **US$10** minimum, bank or PayPal, Tuesday and Thursday runs, and a **US$10 a month** fee from month 7 on a balance left unpaid ([Impact: how partners get paid](https://help.impact.com/en/support/solutions/articles/48001233415-how-do-partners-get-paid-)). Its Partner User Agreement (10 July 2024) has no traffic or live-site clause, bans fake redirects, iframes and scraped leads, requires legally required visitor notices, and restates the FTC rule for creators as disclosure "above the fold" and "at the beginning" ([impact.com Partner User Agreement](https://app.impact.com/content/displaympserviceagreement.ihtml)). Hyatt (secondary) and Skyscanner (primary, but 5,000-visitor floor) are the hotel-side brands found there.

Rakuten's policies (2026-07-13) are the strictest wording read, worth knowing even though no hotel brand was verified there: a disclosure "on every page ... that promotes a product for which you receive any form of compensation", above the fold, beginning with "Disclosure:", in a contrasting colour; a privacy policy naming data, purposes and third parties; self-made links banned; and an inactivity fee after 12 idle months ([Rakuten policies](https://rakutenadvertising.com/legal-notices/affiliate-network-policies/); [Rakuten agreement](https://rakutenadvertising.com/legal-notices/publisher-membership-agreement/)). Sovrn, Skimlinks and FlexOffers add nothing the direct programs lack, and the first two are script products.

## Chains pay on completed stays and only Marriott states its deep-link support

Marriott runs on Partnerize; the FAQ answers the deep-link question directly ("Yes. Marriott supports deep linking"), pays "on the net base sales amount, excluding taxes, service fees, food and beverage" for completed stays, excludes group bookings of 10+ rooms, uses a "7 Day Latency Period", pays bi-weekly above an unstated threshold, and lists rejection reasons including "under-construction sites", "misleading clicks" and "missing privacy policy" ([Marriott affiliate FAQ](https://www.marriott.com/marriott/affiliateprogramfaq.mi), read 2026-09-24). The rate appears only in the console after approval; directories say 3% base (secondary). Hilton's Awin profile (UK; the US program is on CJ, secondary) gives **4%, up to 6% subject to validation, 30-day cookie**, excludes hotels in Belarus, Russia and Ukraine plus SLH, Yotel and Hilton Grand Vacations, bans bidding on "hotel property names", and pays no cashback ([Awin Hilton profile](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/3624)). Accor routes US publishers through CJ, pays "based on the net number of nights booked", accepts "Any website that provides relevant content related to the world of travel", and auto-rejects "websites linked to personal email addresses" ([ALL affiliate page](https://all.accor.com/a/en/affiliate-program.html)). IHG, Hyatt, Radisson, Best Western, Minor/NH and Melia rest on secondary figures because their primary pages were blocked; Wyndham and Choice could not be read at all.

The trade-off is plain. A chain link pays a higher rate on a direct booking and never depends on OTA inventory, but most guide readers book on an OTA, so per-chain links convert less. For this site the sensible order is the OTAs first, then Marriott, Hilton and Accor for the chain properties that already appear among the 15 picks per city.

## Metasearch pays per click and Skyscanner shuts the door on zero traffic

Tripadvisor pays "at least 50% of the commission Tripadvisor receives from its hotel booking partners", on the click-out "regardless of whether or not they book", averaging **US$0.15 to US$0.75 per click-out**, with a 14-day window and roughly a US$50 CJ threshold; every figure is from a snippet of the blocked primary page or a review ([Tripadvisor affiliates, snippet](https://www.tripadvisor.com/affiliates); [Strackr, secondary](https://strackr.com/blog/tripadvisor-affiliate-program)). At that rate a US$50 payout needs about 70 to 330 click-outs, which near-zero traffic will not deliver for many months. trivago (Awin, FlexOffers; per click-out, secondary) and KAYAK (own portal; "clicks, bookings, and ad revenue", rates not public) are the same shape ([KAYAK Affiliates](https://affiliates.kayak.com/)). Skyscanner, on Impact with a 30-day cookie, is the one program with a hard number: traffic "higher than 5,000 unique visitors per month" ([Skyscanner Affiliates](https://partners.skyscanner.net/affiliates)). The site fails it today.

## The compliance work is small, fixed, and the same for every program

The FTC's FAQ tells affiliate sites to "disclose your relationship to the retailer clearly and conspicuously on your site", offers the model wording "I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post", and says "The closer the disclosure is to your recommendation, the better" ([FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). Disclosures 101 adds that they "are likely to be missed if they appear only on an ABOUT ME or profile page, at the end of posts or videos, or anywhere that requires a person to click MORE" ([FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)). The privacy.html sentence is that exact pattern. UK CAP guidance rules out "*affiliate" and "may receive commission" wording and wants the label "upfront and prior to consumer engagement" ([ASA/CAP](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html)); the ASA's reach over a US site is unconfirmed, but the FTC fix satisfies it for free.

Google's spam policy says affiliate links are "not a violation of our policies ... as long as they are qualified with a rel="nofollow" or rel="sponsored" attribute value", and defines thin affiliation as copied merchant descriptions "without any original content or added value" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), 2026-08-28). The site's 893 researched guides pass the second test as long as the hotel cards keep their own text; the links fail the first test until `rel="sponsored"` is added.

On cookies, the ICO guidance (29 April 2026) treats technologies "set on websites that we link to" as the destination site's responsibility, while scripts, pixels and tags loaded on the publisher's own pages are in scope ([ICO storage and access guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/)). Booking sets no affiliate cookie at all; Expedia and Partnerize cookies land on their own domains after the click. So plain links need no banner, and the rule that follows is simple: no partner JavaScript on city pages.

## Conclusion

The decision is less about which program pays most and more about which one a reviewer will approve with no traffic and which one the site can integrate without a script. On both counts the direct OTA programs win: Expedia and Agoda publish their terms, take plain deep links, and state no traffic floor, and Booking via CJ is worth the application even though its 2025 purge shows what happens to small earners later. Stay22's pitch (one account, every OTA) is real, but its economics depend on a claim no source verified, and its main products would undo the no-script, no-banner decision of 2026-09-18.

The larger cost is not legal but editorial: about 13,395 named properties need a partner URL each, and no program documents a "search by name" link. That lookup, stored in citydata with the tracked link in the href and `rel="sponsored"` on the anchor, is the real project. The four copy changes (disclosure line, rel attribute, privacy.html, terms.html) are a single small pass and should ship before the first hotel link, in the same commit that fixes the live Airalo links.
